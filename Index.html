About

Stress is constant in modern life. Most apps respond with instruction — guided breathing, timers, steps to follow. SEI responds with touch. The design challenge was using the universe as a theme: space is vast and overwhelming, but SEI makes it feel physical, intimate, and calm. No instructions. No scores. Just feel it.

The Sensory Loop
Touch → visual response → gentle vibration. Each screen creates its own loop. The phone becomes a tactile tool, not a distraction device.
Comparable Apps
Calm and Headspace rely on audio and cognitive effort. Loona offers visual immersion but limited physical sensation. SEI fills the gap — layered haptics, gesture-first, no reading required.
Haptic Research
Vibration intensity, rhythm, and duration affect emotional perception. Short pulses feel soft. Prolonged vibration increases stress. Every haptic in SEI is designed around this.
Who It's For
Students under pressure, creatives needing focus, urban users, tactile self-regulators — anyone who just wants to feel something.
Accessibility
SEI is designed with accessibility in mind. Every interaction is gesture-based and tactile — no reading required, no cognitive load, no instructions to follow. The haptic system means the app communicates through touch alone, making it usable regardless of visual ability or language. Anyone who can hold a phone can feel it.
Process
Weeks 2–5 · Research & Concept
Researched existing apps — Night Sky, Calm, Headspace, Loona. Found them powerful but either overwhelming or audio-dependent. Shifted focus from educational astronomy to emotional tactile design. The universe became the metaphor, not the subject.
Weeks 6–9 · Build & Midterm Demo
Built 3 functional screens in Xcode. Demo Day was structured in two parts: slides to explain the concept, then handing the phone directly to users. Letting people feel the experience — not just hear about it — was critical for a touch-first project.
Demo Day · User Feedback
Users wanted stronger haptic differences between screens, more dynamic movement on screen 2, and relaxing audio. Multiple people suggested a calm physical environment for the showcase — not just the app. That shifted how I think about presentation entirely.
User Interviews · What Does Calm Mean?
Casually asked people: "what actually feels calming to you?" It turned into informal user research. The key realization — calm means something different to everyone. SEI shouldn't be one fixed experience. It should offer different types of calming interactions so each person finds their own.
Weeks 10–14 · Final Build
Expanded to 5 distinct haptic experiences. Added bubble wrap, haptic planet mixer, spacetime home screen, and per-screen intensity tuning. Replaced orbiting circle with a real planet. Built navigation, home button, and refined every interaction based on user feedback.
Technical Tools
·

Xcode — primary IDE for building and running the iOS app on device

·

SwiftUI — all 5 interactive screens, gestures, animations, and page navigation

·

Core Haptics — custom patterns per screen using CHHapticEngine; transient pulses, looped rhythms, intensity tuning

·

Core Motion — accelerometer data so planets on screen 2 respond to real phone movement

·

Figma — UI/UX wireframes and screen layout before building in Xcode

·

TestFlight — beta testing on real devices throughout development
